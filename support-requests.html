Case study

Finding what was really driving 1,000 help requests a day

For five months, everyone had a different explanation for our support demand. When I pulled every help request we’d logged and analyzed it, the real cause turned out to be one nobody had suggested.

The problem

Our community had about 37 tenured reps, with 6 more coming from training. Communities were growing from a maximum of 40 reps to 48, and leaders were already struggling to keep up at 37 because of how much support the reps needed: roughly 1,000 help requests a day. Reps sent these through help-request forms I had built, which captured the issue, what they had tried, and any resource they had checked.

Five months of explanations

  1. September A recent realignment had shaken everyone up. Reps had bid on new leaders and shifts and teams had changed a lot, so things would settle by October or November.
  2. October–November The Apple launch and Black Friday deals were driving demand.
  3. December Christmas was driving demand.
  4. January Our newest reps were driving it.

What I did

In January I pulled all of our help-request data and looked for every trend I could find:

  • Average requests per day, by day of the week, and by time of day.
  • Requests by rep and by coach.
  • How often reps included a link to the resource document they had checked.
  • How often each issue came up.

What the data showed

  • Mid-tenure reps created the most questions, and had the whole time. Our newer hires actually weren’t asking enough.
  • Six people accounted for most of the questions across the community.
  • Some reps struggled to trust themselves and their resources, and many weren’t using their resources at all.
  • A few real knowledge gaps needed to be addressed.

What changed

I presented the findings to my peer leaders in a community leader meeting. Together we held a community huddle to cover the knowledge gaps and set two new standards: search the resources twice before asking, and include a document link in every help post.

The result

Once we started holding people to the new standards, requests dropped almost immediately, from about 1,000 a day to about 650, roughly 35% fewer.

What I took from it: check the assumption before you build the plan. Four different explanations sounded reasonable, and none of them was right. The data had the answer the whole time.
