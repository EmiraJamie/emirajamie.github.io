Case study

Finding what was really driving 1,000 help requests a day

Everyone expected a big class of new hires to flood our support channel. When I structured the requests and analyzed them, the data showed that tenured employees were driving most of the demand. Retargeting training cut daily requests by about 35%.

The problem

A community of about 40 representatives was sending roughly 1,000 help requests a day. These were questions, tasks that needed a leader’s permissions, and live calls where a rep needed help right away. Most came in as loosely structured messages, so there was no good way to see patterns. A large group of new hires was about to start, and the shared assumption was that they would push demand even higher.

What I did

  • Built structured intake. Separate Microsoft Forms for questions and for live-call help asked for the request type, links to self-help resources, what the rep had already tried, and free-text details.
  • Routed it automatically. Power Automate sent each request into Teams with clear urgency cues, so live-call requests stood out.
  • Analyzed it. I looked at request categories alongside the written comments to find high-volume requesters, recurring knowledge gaps, and places where reps weren’t using the tools they had.
  • Shared it. I took the findings to other coaches and helped shape targeted training and clearer support expectations.

What the data showed

The new hires weren’t the problem. Tenured employees generated the most requests, and mid-tenure reps sent nearly twice as many as new hires. That moved the training focus away from onboarding and toward tool use and specific knowledge gaps across the experienced team.

The result

Daily requests fell from about 1,000 to about 650, roughly 35% fewer. Live-call escalations also became easier to see. The intake tool made the analysis possible, but the drop came from the training and expectations that followed.

What I took from it: check the assumption before you build the plan. If we had trained the new hires harder, we would have spent the effort on the wrong group.
