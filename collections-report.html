Case study

A Power BI report that helped lift collections from about 45% to 75%

Paired with a payment flow my peer leaders and I created, a transaction-level Power BI report let leaders check how calls were being handled and coach within minutes. Collections rose from about 45% to the mid-70s in roughly a month.

The problem

Our team was collecting only about 40–45% of past-due balances on eligible calls. Leaders wanted to coach the calls that mattered most, but the transaction data was hard to work with. It had repeated rows, payments that settled on different days, payment arrangements, reversals, and a messy reporting hierarchy. Finding just two to five calls worth reviewing took a leader more than 30 minutes.

What I did

The fix had two halves that only worked together.

  • A new payment flow. With other leaders, I co-created a top-down payment flow, a consistent way for reps to handle payment conversations.
  • A report to check it. I built a Power BI report that cleaned and modeled the transactions. It showed eligible calls, past-due dollars at stake, payments collected, arrangements, reversals, and calls that ended with no payment. Leaders could view it by rep, coach, or community, and by day, week, or month, with each opportunity linked to its call recording.
  • Fast follow-up. Because the report showed whether each rep was actually following the new flow, leaders could follow up with coaching right away.

The result

Finding the right calls to coach dropped from over 30 minutes to under five. Collections rose more than 10% a week for the first two weeks and kept climbing, reaching the mid-70s by week four. Measured against goal, that’s roughly 70% to over 110%. Over the same three to four weeks, the site moved from last to first in collections. Many leaders and reps contributed to that, and the report was one part of it.

What I took from it: a dashboard on its own rarely changes anything. This one worked because it answered one question, “Is the new flow happening on the calls?”, quickly enough to act on.

This write-up contains no T-Mobile data, screenshots, or internal report names. Figures are approximate results.
