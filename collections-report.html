Case study

A Power BI report that helped lift collections from about 45% to 75%

The standard collections report had the data but buried it. I built a Power BI report around what coaches actually needed. Paired with a new collection approach, it helped lift collections from about 45% to the mid-70s in roughly a month.

The problem

Our team was collecting only about 40–45% of past-due balances on eligible calls. Leaders wanted to coach the calls that mattered most, but the standard transaction report made that hard:

  • Redundant columns cluttered the view, so the important information was hard to separate out.
  • The sorting options didn’t help surface the higher-impact calls worth observing.
  • There was no simple way to find a call in Verint to listen to it.

Finding just two to five calls worth reviewing took a leader more than 30 minutes.

What I did

The fix had two halves that only worked together.

  • A new collection approach. With other leaders, I co-created a top-down collection flow. Reps started with the full balance and worked downward, using assumptive language that expected a payment instead of asking for one.
  • A report built for the coach. My Power BI report cut the clutter and made it easy to sort by call date and spot higher-impact calls. It showed each rep’s collection rate by month, by week ending, and by day, so leaders could see trends and pick out good and bad days. Each call also had a link that opened it directly in Verint, so going from the report to listening to the call took almost no extra steps.
  • Fast follow-up. Because the report showed whether reps were actually using the new approach, leaders could follow up with coaching right away.

The result

Finding the right calls to coach dropped from over 30 minutes to under five. Collections rose more than 10% a week for the first two weeks and kept climbing, reaching the mid-70s by week four. Measured against goal, that’s roughly 70% to over 110%. The site moved from last to the top three in collections. Many leaders and reps contributed to that, and the report was one part of it. Leaders kept using the report until January 2026, when my workspaces were closed after my position was eliminated.

What I took from it: design a report for the person who has to use it. A report full of numbers does nothing if it’s badly designed. Knowing your audience and what they need is what makes a report useful.

Visuals are rebuilt with made-up data and anonymized names, and no real T-Mobile data is shown. Figures in the text are approximate results.
